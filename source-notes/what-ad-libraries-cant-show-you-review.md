# "What Ad Libraries Can't Show You" — evaluation notes

Source: YouTube transcript, the second creator. Twenty client ads shown with spend and
ROAS. Reviewed 2026-10-03 from the transcript only.

## Why it was reviewed

Ad libraries show what runs, not what pays. This video shows a set of ads with their
numbers.

## Concepts worth retaining

From the examples with large, sustained spend:

- A proof point someone could check, paired with a real reason to act now.
- A real person, shot like a normal post, captions burned in.
- Ads that pack variety: every size, colour, use and customer type, answering each
  objection in one piece.
- Catalogue carousels for multi-item businesses; replies to real comments; a founder
  walking through the stock; a best-seller line-up; opening on the price objection; a
  gifting angle that widens who the ad is for.

## What not to import as doctrine

- The small-spend "winners" with very high ROAS. They say little about scale.
- Conclusions drawn from winners only. No losing ads are shown for comparison.
- "Say 'moms' and Meta finds moms" as a mechanism. Calling out the audience is sound
  practice and three sources agree on it; the mechanism is unconfirmed. It can be
  checked in the delivery breakdown by age and gender.
- Urgency lines such as "sold out seven times" unless they are true.

## Conflicts

Cover everything in one ad, against one customer type and one job per ad (Denney). His
own top performers are all real people, while his other video promotes AI video.
