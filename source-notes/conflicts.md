# Where the sources disagree

The hub does not pick a winner by argument. Each disagreement becomes a default plus a
tag or a setting, and each business's results settle it for that business.

| Question | Positions | How the hub handles it |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign structure | One campaign and one ad set with many ads (course). A testing campaign of several small ad sets plus a winners campaign (Schneider). | Default is the single ad set. A separate testing campaign only when it would itself reach about 50 results a week. |
| Using competitor ads | Sort by impressions and long runtime and model them, adapting not copying (course, second creator). Copying competitors is the worst strategy; work from your own gaps (Denney). | Competitor ads are an input. The ledger's origin tag records where each idea came from so hit rates can be compared. |
| One job per ad or cover everything | One customer type, one goal per ad (Denney). Long-running winners that show every option and answer every objection (second creator; the course's local examples lean this way). | Breadth tag: focused or broad. Both are tested. |
| When to cut an ad | At a multiple of the average cost per result (second creator). On the deciding metric, at about 90% confidence, on a schedule; the testing feature shifts budget itself (course). | Default is the course's. The multiple is an optional faster rule, with multiples set per offer. |
| How often to act | Daily checks that pause and shift budget (Meta kit, Schneider). A fixed schedule by conversion volume, one batch (course). | Watch daily for emergencies; act on the schedule. |
| How much creative | Dozens to hundreds of variations (Schneider, second creator). No faster than the last batch can be judged; two great ads beat ten mediocre (course). | Cadence is worked out per offer from time to a confident answer. |
| Test design | Big swings between variations (Denney). One type of change at a time (course). | Both: bold differences between ads, one type of account change per session. |
| Conversion event | Start with an easier, earlier event and move deeper (Schneider). The deepest event you can track (course). | Default is the deepest usable event; an earlier one is an option when volume is too low for the platform to learn. |
| Teaching content | How-to hooks and educational breakdowns are winners (Denney). They attract industry peers, not buyers, for some services (course). | Plays stay in the library with a note; review checks who actually responds. |
| Call-outs | Name the audience and their problem directly (all three main sources). Platform policy limits "you" plus a personal attribute, especially health and money. | Call-out plays pass a policy check before launch. |
| AI people on camera | AI video and AI presenters as a volume source (Schneider, second creator; Denney reports one AI format scaling). The second creator's own top ads are all real people. | AI video is a test lane. Real footage keeps an intake path. AI people are never presented as customers. |
| Meta ordering ads within an ad set | Stated as fact by the second creator and the course. No primary source seen. | Working assumption. It supports keeping formats together, which is the default anyway. |
